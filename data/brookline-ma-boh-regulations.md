**Note:** Brookline is a densely developed, largely sewered inner suburb of Boston (population ~63,000). The town receives its water from the Massachusetts Water Resources Authority (MWRA) and the vast majority of properties are connected to the municipal sewer system, which also discharges to the MWRA system. Septic systems exist only on a very limited number of properties. The town does not maintain a standalone Title 5 addendum or supplementary septic regulation. Properties with on-site septic systems are governed by state Title 5 (310 CMR 15.000) as administered by the Brookline Board of Health.

## Septic System Guide

Source: https://www.brooklinema.gov/1837/Septic-System-Guide (PDF document)

Brookline maintains a Septic System Guide document available through the town's Document Center. The guide provides information for the small number of properties in town that rely on on-site septic systems rather than the municipal sewer. The guide directs property owners to state Title 5 requirements (310 CMR 15.000) for septic system installation, inspection, maintenance, and repair.

### Title 5 Inspection Requirements (State Law)

Per Massachusetts Title 5 (310 CMR 15.301-15.305), septic system inspections are required:
- **At property transfer** (sale of property) - inspection must be completed within 2 years prior to sale
- **When expanding a building** served by a septic system (change in use or increase in flow)
- Inspections must be performed by a System Inspector approved by the Board of Health
- Inspection results: **Pass** (valid 2-3 years depending on pumping schedule), **Conditional Pass**, or **Fail**

### General Maintenance Requirements

- Septic tanks should be pumped every **3-5 years**
- Properties using garbage disposals should pump **annually**
- Inspection reports are submitted to the Board of Health for review and acceptance

## Water & Sewer Replacement Guidelines

Source: https://www.brooklinema.gov/1958/Water-Sewer-Replacement-Guidelines

### Property Owner Responsibilities

- Property owners manage water service pipe replacement under Water & Sewer Division direction
- **Pipes 2" or smaller:** Owner responsibility extends from the curb stop in the sidewalk to the building
- **Private ways:** Owner responsibility extends from the main to the property
- **Pipes exceeding 2":** Owner handles main-to-property replacement, including interior valves
- All pipe size changes fall under owner responsibility

### Contractor Requirements

- Must be bonded and registered with the Town of Brookline
- Responsible for excavation, backfill, and trench restoration from curb stop or main to building foundation
- The Water & Sewer Division installs new service pipe
- Licensed plumbers must connect new pipes to existing building plumbing
- The town does **not** recommend particular contractors but maintains a list of contractors with experience performing work to town standards

## Accessory Dwelling Units (ADUs)

Source: https://www.brooklinema.gov/Faq.aspx?QID=469 ; Brookline Zoning By-Law Section 4.05

### ADU Zoning Requirements

Brookline has updated its ADU bylaw in compliance with the Massachusetts Affordable Homes Act (effective 2025):

- **Allowed by right** on lots with up to 4 residential units
- **Maximum size:** 900 square feet or 50% of the gross floor area of the principal dwelling (whichever is smaller)
- **Design:** Must match the architectural style of the principal dwelling
- **Owner occupancy:** No owner occupancy requirement
- **FAR cap:** The previous 120% FAR cap for ADUs has been eliminated
- **Building code:** Must meet state building code and stretch energy code
- **Attached/within principal dwelling:** Must meet zoning requirements of the principal dwelling under Table 5.01
- **Detached ADUs:** Must comply with requirements applicable to accessory buildings
- **Full zoning details:** Section 4.05 of the Brookline Zoning By-Law

### ADU Sewer/Septic Considerations

- Since Brookline is largely sewered, most ADUs will connect to the municipal sewer system
- A plumbing permit is required for any new plumbing connections
- For the very rare property on septic: the existing septic system must have adequate capacity for the additional flow from the ADU per Title 5 requirements
- Per state Title 5 ADU guidance (MassDEP), if an ADU connects to an existing septic system, the system must incorporate a two-compartment septic tank or two septic tanks in series
- If the ADU is served by its own septic system, a properly sized septic tank is required
- All septic-related ADU work requires Board of Health review of perc tests, system design, and flow calculations

## Well Regulations

Brookline does **not** appear to have adopted local private well regulations. The town receives its water supply from the MWRA, and essentially all properties are served by the municipal water system.

### State Framework

Per Massachusetts General Laws Chapter 111, Section 31, local Boards of Health have authority to regulate private wells. MassDEP does not regulate private wells directly. In the absence of local regulations, the state Private Well Guidelines (MassDEP) apply.

### Key State Requirements

- Private wells are regulated by the local Board of Health under MassDEP guidance
- Water quality testing by a state-certified laboratory is required for new wells
- Title 5 setback requirements apply (50 feet from septic tank, 100 feet from leaching field/cesspool)
- Any irrigation well must have a backflow preventer and cannot be cross-connected with municipal supply

## Summary: Brookline BOH Regulations That Exceed Title 5

Brookline does **not** have a formal Title 5 addendum or supplementary septic regulation. The town is almost entirely sewered through its municipal sewer system connected to the MWRA. Key local provisions:

1. **No local septic regulations beyond Title 5** - Brookline relies entirely on state Title 5 (310 CMR 15.000) for the very limited number of properties with on-site septic systems
2. **Predominantly sewered community** - The vast majority of properties connect to the municipal sewer system operated by the Water & Sewer Division (DPW); very few properties rely on septic systems
3. **Septic System Guide available** - The town maintains a Septic System Guide document (PDF) through the Environmental Health Division for the few remaining septic properties
4. **Plumbing variance fee** - $60 for plumbing variance applications through the Health Department
5. **Water/sewer submetering regulation** - $60 per unit certification fee; water conservation devices required before submetering eligibility (per M.G.L. c. 186, Section 22 and 105 CMR 410.000)
6. **Drainlayer's License required** - $300 annual fee for contractors performing sewer/drain work; must be bonded and registered with the town
7. **Water, Sewer, or Drain Permit required** - For any water, sewer, or drain work; variable fee based on cut size and street age
8. **No local well regulations** - Town is served by MWRA; private wells are uncommon and governed by state guidelines
9. **ADUs allowed by right** - Per the Affordable Homes Act; most ADUs will connect to municipal sewer; rare septic-connected ADUs must comply with state Title 5 requirements
10. **Environmental Health Division** - All septic and wastewater matters are handled by the Chief Sanitarian (Roland Lankah, PhD, MPH, REHS, RS) at 617-730-2303
