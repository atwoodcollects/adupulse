## Rules & Regulations Governing the Subsurface Disposal of Sewage

Source: https://sudbury.ma.us/health/regulations-subsurface-disposal-of-sewage-2/

Adopted under authority of Chapter III Section 31 of the General Laws and amendments, Title V of the State Environmental Code. Originally adopted February 15, 1984; amended February 23, 1989; revised March 30, 1995; amended November 7, 1996 and March 26, 1998.

### Section I - Issuance and Expiration of Sewage Permits
No sewage or waste disposal works shall hereafter be constructed, altered, repaired, or installed until a permit has first been paid for and obtained from the Board of Health. Permits shall become void twelve months after date of issue unless construction thereunder is begun within that period.

### Section II - Suitability of Site
No two sites are alike. If there is any doubt as to the acceptability of a location for subsurface sewage disposal, the Board of Health may require as much additional proof as it feels necessary to remove that doubt. Additional proof may be obtained from, but is not limited to, additional deep test pits and percolation tests, or **waiting until the months of March, April or May to investigate areas of possible severe wetness in the spring.**

### Section III - Leaching Area Requirements
Residential square footage requirements are based on **sidewall area only.**

**Minimum square feet per bedroom:**

| Type | Perc Rate 1-10 min. | Perc Rate over 10-20 min. | Perc Rate over 20-30 min. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trenches | 150 | 170 | 225 |
| Pit or galleries | 50 | 75 | 100 |

**Leaching beds are not permitted.**

When the expansion area is proposed between trenches, the minimum distance (wall to wall) shall be **12 feet**.

**Full service restaurants:** The design requirements will be **twice what is required under Title V** of the State Environmental Code. This includes the size of the septic tanks, grease traps and the leaching facility. Furthermore, alternating pumps must be provided and the effluent must be discharged to two separate disposal areas of equal size. Alarms must be provided in accordance with Title V. Design capacity shall be based upon total indoor and outdoor seating.

### Section IV - Multi-Family Residences (implied from document context)
- Ancillary facilities (function halls, laundry rooms, exercise facilities, etc.) shall be considered additional sources of sewage flow
- Leaching area requirements for multi-family shall meet Section III requirements
- Minimum daily sewage design flow based on number of rooms usable as bedrooms irrespective of ownership or contents of a Master Deed for condominiums

**Bedroom definition:** A room providing privacy, intended primarily for sleeping, consisting of:
- a. Floor space of no less than 70 square feet
- b. For new construction, ceiling height of no less than 7 feet 3 inches
- c. For existing housing and mobile homes, ceiling height of no less than 7 feet 0 inches
- d. An electrical service and ventilation
- e. At least one window or slider

**Bedroom count presumptions for multi-family:** Units presumed to have at least one bedroom up to 5 rooms (not counting bathrooms, hallways, unheated storage). Six or seven rooms = at least two bedrooms. Eight or more rooms = at least three bedrooms. Units exceeding 1,200 sq ft = minimum of two bedrooms calculated. The higher calculation shall be used.

**B. Minimum Sewage Design Flow for "Housing for the Elderly":**
- One bedroom units: **180 gallons per day** (rather than 200 gpd)
- Additional bedrooms: **110 gallons per day per bedroom**

### Section V - Construction in Fill
When construction in clean granular fill is required, **both the primary area and the expansion area must be prepared.**

### Section VI - Pumps
**Sewage pumps of any type are hereby prohibited** from any residential or other dwelling unit's system unless a variance is granted by the Board of Health.

### Section VII - Setbacks from Watercourses (implied section number)
Surface watercourses shall be defined as any area where water is present on the surface of the ground for a period of three months. This includes, but is not limited to: marshes, wetlands, swamps, rivers, streams, brooks, and ponds. Vegetated wetlands and drainage swales that are temporarily active due to precipitation are excluded from this definition.

**Leaching facilities must be a minimum of fifty (50) feet from all other watercourses** that are deemed wetlands because of vegetation and/or soil conditions.

Whichever requirement maximizes the distance between watercourses and leaching facilities will be enforced.

### Section VIII - Installation in Aquifer Protection Areas
If a subsurface disposal system is to be installed in a **Zone II aquifer protection area**, the distance from the bottom of the leaching facility to the high groundwater must be **six (6) feet**.

### Section IX - Subsurface Drains
When a drain is proposed to lower the groundwater so the bottom of the leaching facility will be four feet above the water table, it must first be installed and proved to be functioning prior to the Board of Health issuing a permit.

### Section X - Retaining Walls
When retaining walls are proposed in lieu of the slope requirement as detailed in Title V of the State Environmental Code, **a variance is needed from the Sudbury Board of Health.**

### Section XI - Foundations
All foundations must be properly engineered and constructed to prevent chronic dampness from occurring in the building.

### Section XII - Septic System Location
A subsurface disposal system must be on the **same lot** as the dwelling or buildings it serves.

### Section XIII - Sewage System Inspection Requirements
It shall be the responsibility of the installer to arrange with the Board of Health for the following inspections:

A. Any clean granular fill that is required must first be approved by the Board of Health or their agent prior to it being installed.
B. After excavation of leaching area.
C. After placement of fill when system is to be constructed in same.
D. After construction is complete but before backfilling.
E. After construction is complete, the Board requires a written certification from a Registered Professional Engineer.

### Section XIV - Provision of Toilet Facilities for Places of Business
A. The owner of any existing building hereafter erected or converted into one or more stores or other places of business shall provide sufficient toilet facilities as determined by the Board of Health.
B. The owner of any building or space erected or converted into one or more food service establishments with any seating (indoor or outdoor) which serves the public shall provide toilet facilities for patrons of each individual establishment.

### Section XV - Disposal of Septage
All septage pumped in the Town of Sudbury must be disposed of at the **Wayland-Sudbury Septage Treatment Facility**, or under emergency conditions at a facility designated by the Board of Health or the Wayland Road Commissioners. Failure to comply will subject the pumper to the revocation of his permit to pump in Sudbury.

### Section XVI - Variances
No provision of these regulations may be varied except by a vote of the Board of Health. Such variances will be granted only when the Board believes it to be in the interest of protecting the public health. **Neither financial hardship nor past practices are adequate reasons** for the granting of variances.

## In-Ground Irrigation Systems Regulations (Article XXVII)

Source: https://sudbury.ma.us/boardofhealth/?attachment_id=182

Revised effective 4/4/06.

### Key Requirements:
- Installation permitted **only when the source is a private well** owned by the property owner
- All wells tested for coliform bacteria; treatment required if positive
- **Setbacks:** Private wells for irrigation shall not be located within:
  - **100 feet** of a sewage disposal system
  - **100 feet** of an existing potable water supply well
  - **100 feet** of a wetland or vernal pool
  - Lesser setbacks to sewage disposal systems may be approved by BOH
- Wells drilled to **minimum depth of 100 feet** unless hydrogeological analysis demonstrates cone of influence does not intercept surface water
- **No connection** between private water supply and municipal water service (separation using valves or removable pipe sections prohibited)
- All irrigation systems shall utilize **moisture sensors**
- Integrated Pest Management Plan required with application

## Summary: Sudbury BOH Regulations That Exceed Title 5

1. **Leaching beds prohibited** - Only trenches and pit/galleries allowed
2. **Increased leaching area requirements** - 150-225 sq ft per bedroom for trenches (based on perc rate), vs Title 5 standard
3. **12-foot minimum trench separation** (wall to wall) for expansion areas
4. **Full service restaurants: twice Title V requirements** - Double size of septic tanks, grease traps, and leaching; alternating pumps; two separate disposal areas; alarms required
5. **Sewage pumps prohibited** unless variance granted by BOH
6. **50-foot setback from watercourses/wetlands** for leaching facilities
7. **6-foot separation to groundwater in Zone II** aquifer protection areas (vs Title 5 standard of 4 feet)
8. **Retaining walls require variance** if proposed in lieu of Title V slope requirements
9. **Subsurface drains must be installed and functioning** before permit issuance
10. **Both primary and expansion areas must be prepared** when construction in fill is required
11. **Registered Professional Engineer certification** required after construction
12. **Septage disposal restricted** to Wayland-Sudbury Septage Treatment Facility
13. **Spring testing may be required** (March-May) for questionable sites
14. **Irrigation well setbacks** - 100 feet from septic, wells, and wetlands; minimum 100-foot depth
15. **Housing for the Elderly** reduced design flow: 180 gpd for 1BR (vs 200), 110 gpd/additional bedroom
16. **Variances only for public health reasons** - financial hardship and past practices are not adequate
