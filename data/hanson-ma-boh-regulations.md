## Supplemental Title V Regulations (Revised August 29, 2023)

**To:** All Title 5 Inspectors
**From:** Board of Health
**Date:** August 21, 2012
**Re:** Title 5 Inspectors
**Revised:** August 29, 2023

Title 5 Inspector's must be permitted by the Hanson Board of Health before they are to work in the Town of Hanson. The fee for this permit is $100.00. **Permits are valid from the time of application through December 31st of that year.**

Title 5 Inspection reports will not be accepted unless you are permitted in Hanson. Each inspection must follow the regulations below:

1. **Single cesspools automatically fail.**

2. *If the percolation test, on the property that is having the inspection, was done prior to May 1995 or there is no record of a percolation on file at the Hanson BOH, then a soil evaluation must be performed by a licensed S.E.* All systems must be soil evaluated to a depth greater than the lowest elevation of the leaching area.

3. The system must be pumped after the inspection. The integrity of the tank/cesspool must be confirmed at this time. *Pumping records must be submitted attached to the report.*

4. Page 7 of the report (under flow conditions) should have the water usage calculated in gallons per day. *Water meter readings must be submitted attached to the report.*

5. Page 15 of the report must be the Inspector's sketch, and the SAS should be shown if possible.

6. Outlet cover of septic tank must be brought to grade. In addition, a filter must be placed on the outlet of the tank. D-box cover will be brought to within 6" of grade. *A repair permit will be required for a fee of $100.00.*

7. *Inspectors will conduct a room count inside the house* to determine the number of bedrooms as defined in Title 5.

8. All Title V inspections must include exposing all Title V components. Photographs of all exposed components must be attached to the Title V report. Using an underground camera or scope will only be permitted with prior approval from the Hanson Board of Health.

9. If State and/or local requirements cannot be met, the report should be classified as a "conditional pass" or "needs further evaluation by the BOH".

10. *Reports must be submitted within 15 days of the inspection.*

11. A fee of $25.00 (Payable to the Town of Hanson) must be submitted with each Title V Inspection Report.

(Each regulation above shall be construed as separate to the end that if any regulation, sentence, clause or phase thereof shall be held invalid for any reason, the remainder of the regulation shall remain in full force and effect.)

## Submitting a Septic Plan for Approval

### Requirements
When submitting a Septic Plan for approval please provide the following:
- Application for Disposal Works Construction
- 4 sets of plans, all must have engineer's signature, engineer's stamp, one with the engineer's wet stamp
- All soil information
- Any Conservation filings
- Fee

Once all items are submitted, it is forwarded to our outside consultant. The plans will be reviewed and returned to the BOH. If changes need to be made, the review sheet will be forwarded to you for plan revisions. Four new sets of stamped plans and resubmission fee need to be submitted with requested changes.

## Septic System Tips to Avoid Trouble

**DO:**
- Have your tank pumped out and system inspected every 1 to 2 years by a licensed septic contractor.
- Keep a record of pumping, inspections and other maintenance.
- Practice water conservation. Repair dripping faucets and leaking toilets, run washing machines and dishwashers only when full, avoid long showers and use water-saving features in faucets, showerheads and toilets.
- Learn the location of your septic system and leaching field. Keep a sketch of it handy for service visits. If your system has a flow diversion valve, learn its location and turn it once a year. Flow diverters can add many years to the life of your system.
- Divert roof drains and surface water from driveways and hillsides away from the septic system. Keep sump pumps and house footing drains away from the septic system.
- Take leftover hazardous household chemicals to your approved hazardous waste collection center for disposal. Use bleach, disinfectants and drain and toilet bowl cleaners sparingly and in accordance with product labels.

**DON'T:**
- Allow anyone to drive or park over any part of the system. The area over the leach field should be left undisturbed with only a mowed grass cover. Roots from nearby trees or shrubs may clog and damage your drain lines.
- Make or allow repairs to your septic system without obtaining the required health department permit. Use professional licensed septic contractors when needed.
- Use your toilet as a trash can by dumping nondegradables down your toilet or drains. Also, don't poison your septic system and the groundwater by pouring harmful chemicals down the drain. They can kill the beneficial bacteria that treat your wastewater.

**Keep the following materials out of your septic system:**
- NONDEGRADABLES: Grease - Disposable Diapers - Plastics
- POISONS: Gasoline - Oil - Paint - Paint Thinner - Pesticides - Antifreeze

## Well Regulations (Adopted April 10, 2007)

### I. Purpose
These regulations are intended to protect the public health and general welfare by ensuring that private wells are constructed in a manner which will protect the quality of the groundwater derived from private wells.

### II. Authority
These regulations are adopted by the Hanson Board of Health, as authorized by Massachusetts General Laws, Chapter 111, section 31. These regulations supersede all previous regulations adopted by the Board of Health pursuant to the construction of private wells.

### III. Definitions
- **Agent:** Any person designated and authorized by the Board to execute these regulations.
- **Applicant:** Any person who intends to have a private well constructed for drinking purposes.
- **Aquifer:** A water bearing geologic formation that contains sufficient saturated permeable material to yield significant quantities of water to wells and springs.
- **Bentonite Grout:** A mixture of bentonite (API Standard 13A) and water in a ratio of not less than one pound of bentonite per gallon of water.
- **Board:** The Board of Health of Hanson, Massachusetts or its authorized agent.
- **Private Well:** Any dug, driven, or drilled hole, with a depth greater than its largest surface diameter developed to supply water intended and/or used for human consumption and not subject to regulation by 310 CMR 22.00.

### IV. Well Construction Permit
The property owner or designated representative shall obtain a permit from the Board of Health prior to the commencement of construction of a private well.

Each permit application shall include:
1. Property owner's name and address
2. Well driller's name and proof of valid state registration
3. A plan prepared by an engineer with a specified scale, showing location of the proposed well in relation to existing or proposed structures, illustrating visible prior and current land uses within 200 feet of the proposed well location
4. Proof that abutting property owners have been notified
5. A permit fee of $100.00

Permits expire one year from date of issuance.

### V. Water Supply Certificate
A Water Supply Certificate must be issued for the use of a private well prior to the issuance of an occupancy permit for an existing structure or prior to the issuance of a building permit for new construction.

### VI. Well Location and Use Requirements (Key Setback Requirements)
- **Property line:** At least 10 feet from any property line
- **Adjacent structure:** Centerline of well shall clear any projection from adjacent structure by at least 5 feet
- **Minimum depth:** Top of intake screen minimum depth of 25 feet below surface
- **Public roadway:** Minimum 25 feet from normal driving surface or 15 feet from road right-of-way, whichever is greater
- **Surface water:** At least 25 feet laterally from normal high water mark of any lake, pond, river, stream, ditch, or slough
- **Building sewer (watertight):** Minimum 10 feet
- **Building sewer (other):** Minimum 50 feet
- **Septic tank:** Minimum 50 feet
- **Leaching field:** Minimum 100 feet
- **Privy:** Minimum 100 feet
- **Water supply lines vs sewer lines:** At least 10 feet from and 18 inches above any sewer line

### VII. Water Quantity Requirements
- Pump tests: 4 hours minimum, producing at least 1,200 gallons
- Recommended: additional 100 gallons per hour per bedroom in excess of three

### VIII. Water Quality Testing Requirements
Testing by EPA or Massachusetts certified laboratory for:

**TABLE ONE - Analytical Requirements for New Potable Wells:**
1. Inorganics: arsenic, lead, nitrate plus nitrite nitrogen, chloride, copper, fluoride, hardness, iron, manganese, sodium, pH
2. Volatile organic compounds by EPA 524.2 (plus MTBE)
3. Semi-volatile organic compounds by EPA 504, 508, 525, 531, and 515
4. Coliform bacteria
5. Radionuclides: Radon, Gross alpha activity (bedrock wells only)

**TABLE TWO - Requirements at Property Transfer:**
1. Inorganics (same as above)
2. Volatile organic compounds by EPA 524.2 (plus MTBE)
3. Coliform bacteria

### IX. Well Construction Requirements
- Must use steel or thermoplastic casing
- Wells must be completed above grade, casing extending at least 12 inches above finished ground surface
- Bedrock wells must be grouted from top of weathered rock interface to 15 feet into competent bedrock
- All wells must have surface seal below local frost line

### X-XIX. Enforcement, Hearings, Appeals, Penalties, Variance, Severability, Effective Date, Disclaimer

**Penalties:** Fine of not less than $10 nor more than $500 per violation. Each day's failure to comply constitutes separate violation.

**Variance:** Board may grant variance after public hearing when enforcement would do manifest injustice and equivalent protection will be provided.

## General Bylaw Article 3-1 - Board of Health

### Section 1
No person shall be permitted to throw into or leave in or upon any Street, Court, Square, Lane, Alley, Public Square, Public Enclosure, Pond, Body of Water, or Vacant Lot, within the Town limits, materials that would be offensive or injurious to health without the license of the Board of Health.

### Section 2
No person shall deposit upon any dump, street or way or upon any public or private property: any garbage, food, containers, decayed meats, fish, fruits, vegetables or any substance which may decompose. Persons desiring to make such material disposal shall submit in writing to the Board of Health, a plan indicating the disposal site.

### Section 3
Violations: fine not exceeding $200 for first offense and not more than $1,000 for each subsequent offense (Chapter 270, Section 16 of the General Laws).

### Section 4
Wildlife feeding restrictions (adopted 5/03).

## Summary: Hanson BOH Regulations That Exceed or Supplement Title 5

### Key Local Requirements Beyond State Title 5:

1. **Single cesspools automatically fail** (Title 5 inspection)
2. **Soil evaluation required** if perc test was done before May 1995 or no record on file - must be performed by licensed S.E.
3. **System must be pumped after inspection** with pumping records submitted
4. **Water meter readings required** with inspection reports
5. **Outlet cover must be brought to grade**; filter required on outlet of tank; D-box cover within 6" of grade
6. **Room count required inside house** during inspection
7. **All Title V components must be exposed** during inspection; photographs of all exposed components required; underground cameras/scopes only with prior BOH approval
8. **Reports must be submitted within 15 days**
9. **$25 fee** per Title V Inspection Report
10. **$100 permit fee** for Title 5 Inspectors to work in Hanson
11. **Well setbacks from septic components:** 50 feet from septic tank, 100 feet from leaching field (local well regulations)

### Permit Fees:
- Title 5 Inspector permit: $100/year
- Title 5 Inspection Report fee: $25/report
- Repair permit (for outlet/D-box work): $100
- Disposal Works Construction: $200-$800+ (residential), $250-$300+ (business)
- Septic Component Replacement: $100
